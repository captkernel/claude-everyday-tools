## Focus & executive function

### 1. Task-paralysis shatterer
*When to use: you have the time and the task but physically can't start.*

> I am staring at [TASK] and can't start. Break this down into "ridiculously small" steps that each take less than 1 minute. Give me only the first step, and tell me exactly where to put my hands to begin.

### 2. Dopamine menu
*When to use: you're under-stimulated and bouncing off the walls instead of working.*

> I'm feeling under-stimulated. Create a "dopamine menu" for me with 5-minute "appetizers" (quick movement), 20-minute "entrees" (deep work), and 10-minute "sides" (creative play) to keep my brain engaged.

### 3. Body-doubling check-in
*When to use: you focus better when someone is "there" expecting an update.*

> Act as my virtual body double for the next [DURATION, e.g. 30 minutes]. I'll tell you what I'm working on. Check in every [INTERVAL, e.g. 10 minutes] to ask for a status update and keep my focus anchored.

### 4. Context-switch palate cleanser
*When to use: you finished one task and your brain is stuck before the next.*

> I just finished [TASK A] and need to start [TASK B], but my brain is stuck. Design a 3-minute "mental palate cleanser" routine to help me transition between these two different types of energy.

### 5. Gamify a boring task
*When to use: an administrative chore you keep avoiding.*

> I have a boring administrative task: [TASK]. Help me gamify it by connecting it to my current hyper-fixation: [INTEREST]. Create a "quest" structure where finishing the task unlocks a reward.

### 6. Time-mapping (time-blindness audit)
*When to use: you chronically underestimate how long things take.*

> I think [PROJECT] will take [YOUR ESTIMATE], but it usually takes much longer. Help me "time-map" it by identifying the 3 hidden sub-tasks I always forget to account for, so I can set a realistic deadline.

### 7. Open-loop dump
*When to use: your head is full of nagging worries and nothing is getting done.*

> My brain is full of open loops. I'll dump everything I'm worried about below. Categorize these into "Now," "Later," and "Trash," then write a one-sentence actionable next step for only the "Now" items.
>
> [DUMP EVERYTHING HERE]

## Accelerated learning

### 1. 4-hour crash plan
*When to use: you need to be functional in a new skill fast, not exhaustively schooled.*

> You are a teacher who only has 4 hours with me and will never see me again. Your only objective is to make me functional in [SKILL] before the time runs out. Don't give me theory without practical use. Don't give me a list. Tell me: what to learn first, what to completely ignore, and what single exercise — if I do it once — will already put me ahead of most people who've been studying this for months.

### 2. Error-simulation Socratic loop
*When to use: you learn a concept best by making mistakes and being questioned, not lectured.*

> Don't explain [CONCEPT] to me. Put me directly into a real situation where I'd have to use it and would probably make a mistake. When I make a mistake, don't give me the answer — ask me a question that forces me to discover where my reasoning is broken. Only give me the answer after I've tried at least twice. Repeat this cycle until I can get it right without hesitation.

### 3. One-sentence-unlock explainer
*When to use: a piece of content is confusing and you want the keystone idea first.*

> The content below is confusing to me. Before explaining it, tell me: what is the one sentence that, if I understand it, will make the rest make sense on its own? Explain only that sentence first, using an everyday analogy with no technical terms. Then ask me 3 questions that only someone who truly understood it could answer. Don't continue until I pass all three.
>
> [PASTE THE CONTENT HERE]

### 4. Goal-anchored 7-day path
*When to use: you want a concrete result by a deadline, not "learning the skill" in general.*

> My real goal is [GOAL]. It's not to learn [SKILL] in general — it's to achieve [SPECIFIC RESULT] within [DEADLINE]. I already know [WHAT YOU ALREADY MASTER]. Based on that, build me a 7-day learning path. Each day must include: one single task that fits within 45 minutes, a clear criterion so I know whether I did it correctly, and what NOT to do that day so I don't waste time. If the path doesn't lead me to the goal, rebuild it until it does.

### 5. Hidden-gap detector
*When to use: you think you've mastered something and want your blind spots exposed.*

> I think I already master [SKILL]. I want you to prove me wrong. Ask me 5 questions that seem simple but expose the gaps of someone who has never truly gone deep. For every answer I give, tell me what it reveals about what's still missing in my foundation. Don't go easy on me. If I'm being shallow, tell me directly.

### 6. Forced Feynman
*When to use: right after studying, to find out what you only think you understood.*

> I just studied [TOPIC]. I'm going to explain what I understood as if you were a 10-year-old child. While I explain, stop me every time I use jargon without knowing what it means, skip a step in the reasoning, or simplify so much that it becomes wrong. At the end, tell me exactly what these mistakes reveal about what's still not solid in my mind.
